World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia and Ukraine closed two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi with an agreement in principle on a large prisoner-of-war exchange — Kyiv had proposed 1,000 to 1,500 prisoners per side, with final numbers due Thursday — but no ceasefire and no deal on territory, in their first meeting in nine months (AP).

The COP31 leaders' summit opened in Antalya, Turkey, with 102 heads of state and government gathered for two days on climate finance; UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told them the world's slow response has put it on a path to destruction (AFP).

President Donald Trump said the war with Iran is "militarily effectively over," while Iran's leadership refused to surrender any enriched uranium and said the Strait of Hormuz, closed for over 250 days, will never return to its pre-war status (Al Jazeera).

The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots across the United States and Europe, including one against RAF Fairford in England (New York Times).

Israeli strikes killed at least seven people in Gaza, among them a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said.

Four million workers took part in Spain's first general strike since 2012, called by the UGT and CCOO unions over housing costs — average rent, at 1,176 euros, nearly equals a young worker's monthly pay — ahead of a snap election on 29 November (Reuters).

Trump said he expects to meet China's Xi Jinping, and possibly Russia's Vladimir Putin, at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18-19 November (Afterlight News).

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, beginning the 42-day countdown to an Ebola-free declaration (One Turn News).

In Chile, rare rain carpeted nearly 7,000 square miles of the Atacama Desert in its biggest wildflower bloom in decades (NPR).
